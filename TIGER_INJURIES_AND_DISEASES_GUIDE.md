# Tiger Injuries and Diseases — External Indicative Signs

A detailed reference document covering common injuries, diseases, and conditions affecting tigers (*Panthera tigris*), with the external and visible signs used to identify them in the field, from patrol reports, or through camera-trap imagery.

## 2. Traumatic Injuries

### Snare and Trap Wounds
Snares are among the most lethal threats to wild tigers in habitat corridors and fringe zones:
- Deep circumferential lacerations around the neck, leg, or torso where wire or rope has cut into tissue
- Severe distal edema (swelling distal to the constriction due to impaired venous/lymphatic return)
- Necrotic (blackened, devitalized) tissue or open suppurative wounds with foul discharge
- Embedded wire, rope, or cable still visible on the body
- Partial or complete limb amputation in advanced or chronic cases
- Persistent non-weight-bearing lameness or three-legged gait
- Self-mutilation injuries from the tiger biting at the snare to free itself

### Territorial Fight Wounds
Adult tigers, especially territorial males, sustain frequent trauma from intraspecific conflicts:
- Deep claw lacerations across the facial crest, neck, shoulders, and flanks
- Puncture wounds from canine bites, commonly around the cranial vault, neck, and forelimbs
- Torn, notched, or partially missing ear pinnae
- Fresh open lacerations with active hemorrhage versus older scarred wounds with alopecic healing
- Subcutaneous abscess formation at bite sites due to anaerobic bacterial inoculation

### Poaching-Related Trauma
- Gunshot wounds: circular entry and ragged exit punctures, lead/bullet fragments, surrounding ecchymosis
- Leg-hold trap trauma: crush injuries, severed distal digits, compound fractures
- Blunt force trauma: extensive hematomas, asymmetrical swelling

### Skeletal Fractures
- Visible limb deformity, abnormal angular deviation, or crepitus
- Complete non-weight-bearing lameness
- Dragging of a limb while walking
- Marked swelling around long bones (femur, humerus, radius/ulna) or joints
- Guarding behavior and prolonged recumbency

### Porcupine Quill Penetrations
Common during predatory encounters with Indian crested porcupines (*Hystrix indica*):
- Barbed quills visibly embedded in the muzzle, lips, buccal mucosa, forepaws, or chest
- Severe localized inflammation, cellulitis, and draining sinus tracts
- Secondary abscessation if quills fragment or undergo retrograde migration into deep fascia
- Starvation and dehydration resulting from oral pain and inability to grasp prey

### Vehicle and Railway Collisions
Frequent in fragmented landscapes bisected by linear transport infrastructure:
- Severe asymmetrical blunt force impact trauma
- Open contaminated wounds embedded with ballast stone or road debris
- Pelvic fractures or spinal cord transection leading to dragged hindquarters and paraplegia

## 3. Infectious and Emerging Pathogens

### Canine Distemper Virus (CDV)
- Mucopurulent nasal and bilateral ocular discharge
- Pathognomonic loss of natural wariness toward humans, vehicles, or villages
- Severe neurological signs: ataxia, head pressing, disorientation, myoclonus (involuntary muscle twitches), and grand mal seizures
- Progressive emaciation and hyperkeratosis of digital footpads ("hard pad")

### Feline Panleukopenia / Parvovirus
- Severe watery or bloody enteritis with soiled perineal fur
- Intractable vomiting and hyperacute dehydration
- Rapid loss of body condition and severe leukopenia-induced lethargy

### Bovine Tuberculosis (*Mycobacterium bovis*)
- Chronic non-productive or productive coughing
- Labored, rapid, or open-mouth breathing
- Progressive emaciation despite feeding opportunities; prominent dorsal vertebrae and pelvic bones

### Rabies Virus
- Marked behavioral alterations: erratic aggression, loss of fear, hydrophobia
- Hypersalivation, frothing at the oral cavity, inability to swallow
- Ascending lower motor neuron paralysis starting in the hindlimbs

### Anthrax (*Bacillus anthracis*)
- Acute fatal septicemia contracted from scavenged livestock carcasses
- Rapid swelling of the cervical, pharyngeal, and submandibular tissues
- Unclotted dark bloody discharge from the oral, nasal, and anal orifices

## 4. Anomaly Detection Indicators (Field Screening)

- **Morphological Anomalies**: Asymmetrical body contours, unexplained non-traumatic swellings, focal coat discoloration.
- **Gait Anomalies**: Irregular stride cadence, persistent head tilt, scuffing of dorsal paw surfaces.
- **Behavioral-Visual Anomalies**: Diurnal activity inversion, lingering at camera trap stations, repetitive unilateral licking/grooming.
- **Surface & Texture Anomalies**: Atypical alopecia patterns, hyperkeratotic plaques, subcutaneous nodular masses.
